02 — Type

In context

Choosing a size

Most of the time the answer is --text-body. Reach past it only when a piece of text has a different job to do: a page has one thing to announce, a section has one thing to name, and a control has one word on it. Everything else is prose, and prose is 17 pixels.

When something feels like it needs its own size, look again at what it's doing. A caption is a label. A pull quote is a lead. A card title is a heading at body size. The scale is short on purpose, and almost every page fits inside it.

When you're tempted anyway

Three moves cover nearly every case without adding a size:

  • Set it in medium weight to pull it forward.
  • Set it in --fg-muted to push it back.
  • Give it more space around it, which does more than any size change.

Muted text is the quiet register — footnotes, timestamps, the sentence under a heading. It stays the same size, so the rhythm of the page doesn't change.

03 — Space

Fibonacci, in pixels.

3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55, 89, 144, 233. The gaps widen the way they do in the series, so small things sit close and big things get real room.
